# LGN_snnTorch_classification
A spiking neural network of the lateral geniculate nucleus developed on snnTorch running on GPU and demonstrating image classification task

'A Brain-inspired Neural Network for Image Classification' is accepted for oral presentation at IETCINT 2025 on 23rd August and will be published in IEEE Xplore.

Abstract:
We have presented for the first time a neuromorphic network that performs image classification task successfully. The thalamus is a crucial deep brain structure that receives sensory information for processing and communicates this to the neocortex, the seat of intelligence in our brain, for perception and subsequent decisions and actions. However, replicating the very large-scale networks of the brain is challenging, not to mention the requirement of high-energy resources. Current emphasis is on building sustainable technology. In this work, we have implemented a spiking neural network of the thalamus using the recently proposed snnTorch software library that extends the capabilities of PyTorch and successfully implements gradient-based learning for spiking neural networks; tensor data structure facilitates parallel computation on GPUs. Our work demonstrates that small-scale spiking neural networks mimicking deep-brain recurrent architectures and simulated on the snnTorch platform are successful in categorising images of the MNIST and F-MNIST datasets with high accuracy of 93.72% and 74.34% respectively even in the presence of background noise.
